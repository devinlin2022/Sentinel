Part I - Terminologies

Foundational concepts: Cost Models, Incoterms, and Core Surcharges.

1. Core Concepts: Cost Model (CM) and Incoterms® 2020

These two mechanisms define operational scope and financial liability for every shipment.

At a Glance: Two Sides of a Shipment

While both dictate financial responsibilities, they govern fundamentally different relationships. Understanding this distinction is key to managing operations and costs effectively.

Cost Model (CM)

Defines the relationship between Colgate Palmolive (The Company) and the Freight Forwarder (FF).

CP Company ↔ Freight Forwarder

Purpose: To define the FF's scope of service and provide a benchmark for controlling freight spend.

Incoterms® 2020

Defines the relationship between the CP Shipper (Seller) and the CP Consignee (Buyer).

CP Shipper ↔ CP Consignee

Purpose: To determine the precise point where costs and, critically, the risk of loss/damage, transfer.

Colgate-Palmolive Cost Models (CM)

These internal models define the exact physical services the Freight Forwarder is paid to perform.

DD (Door to Door)

🏭 → 🚚 → 🚢 → 🚚 → 🏢

FF manages the ENTIRE journey, from origin pick-up to final destination delivery.

DP (Door to Port)

🏭 → 🚚 → 🚢

FF manages origin pick-up and ocean freight. Consignee handles destination logistics.

PD (Port to Door)

🚢 → 🚚 → 🏢

Shipper handles origin logistics. FF manages ocean freight and destination delivery.

PP (Port to Port)

🚢

FF manages ONLY the ocean freight leg. Both Shipper and Consignee handle their local logistics.

Incoterms® 2020: Detailed Service Scope

These eleven internationally recognized terms define the allocation of responsibility, costs, and risk transfer between the Seller (Shipper) and Buyer (Consignee).

EXW (Ex Works)

Minimal Seller Obligation. Goods are made available at the seller's premises. Buyer bears all subsequent costs and risks from that point.

FCA (Free Carrier)

Seller delivers goods to the buyer's named carrier at a specified place. Risk and cost transfer immediately upon delivery to the carrier.

CPT (Carriage Paid To)

Seller pays for carriage to the named destination. Risk transfers upon delivery to the first carrier (separation of risk and cost points).

CIP (Carriage & Insurance Paid To)

Same as CPT, but Seller must also procure minimum insurance cover (Clause A) for the Buyer's risk of loss or damage.

DAP (Delivered at Place)

Seller delivers goods ready for unloading at the named destination. Seller bears all risks until the cargo is ready for unloading.

DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded)

Seller delivers goods, unloaded, at the named destination. Seller bears all costs and risks, including associated unloading fees.

DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)

Maximum Seller Obligation. Seller is responsible for delivering the goods, cleared for import, and all duties paid to the buyer's destination.

FAS (Free Alongside Ship) - Sea Only

Seller delivers goods alongside the vessel at the port of shipment. Risk and cost transfer at this point.

FOB (Free On Board) - Sea Only

Seller delivers goods on board the vessel at the port of shipment. Risk and cost transfer immediately when goods are aboard.

CFR (Cost and Freight) - Sea Only

Seller pays costs and freight to the named port of destination. Risk transfers when goods are on board the vessel (at origin).

CIF (Cost, Insurance & Freight) - Sea Only

Same as CFR, but Seller also procures minimum insurance cover (Clause C) against the Buyer's risk of loss or damage.

Synthesis of Key Distinction

Effective supply chain execution requires managers to recognize the independent function of these two critical instruments.

Cost Model (CM)

A Contractual Mechanism dictating the FF's Scope of Service and defining what the Company is invoiced for the transport leg.

Incoterm

A Commercial Regulation defining the legal point of Cost Allocation and Risk Transfer between the Seller and Buyer country teams.

The Overlap

A PP Cost Model on a CFR Incoterm means the Shipper pays the FF for the ocean freight, but must internally manage and pay for the local pre-carriage services.

2. Surcharges and Free Time Management

Managing BAF fluctuations and minimizing detention/demurrage charges are primary cost control responsibilities.

Bunker Adjustment Factor (BAF)

Definition & Purpose:

BAF is a fluctuating ocean freight surcharge designed to compensate the carrier for changes in the cost of fuel (bunker fuel). It is adjusted mechanically on a quarterly basis.

CP's Calculation & Management:

  • BAF is mechanically adjusted at the start of each quarter (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4).
  • Adjustment is based on the relative change in the Bunker World Index (BWI).
  • Calculation compares the average BWI rate of the past 3 months against the prior 3-month period.
  • Colgate Palmolive updates the BAF rate within the first two weeks of the month before the quarter applies (e.g., mid-September for Q4).

BAF Timeline Example (Q4):

April - June
July - September
October - December (Q4 Rate Effective)

BAF rate for Q4 is calculated based on the % change of average BWI from (July-Sept) vs. (April-June).

Detention, Demurrage & Free Time

Demurrage (Terminal Time)

Fees charged when import containers are full and under the control of the shipping line or terminal at the port/terminal beyond the agreed Demurrage Free Time.

Location: Inside the terminal/port.

Detention (Trucking Time)

Fees charged when the Consignee holds onto the carrier's container (empty or full) outside of the port, terminal, or depot beyond the agreed Detention Free Time.

Location: Outside the terminal (e.g., CP warehouse).

3. Standard Global Freight Forwarding Agreement Summary

The core contractual relationship dictates services, performance, and billing for the FF.

3.1. Freight Forwarder Services and Requirements

Core Operational Services:

  • Providing Ocean Freight Services and ancillary ground services (as required by the CM).
  • Managing export/import documentation and customs clearance services upon request.
  • Acting as an independent contractor, arranging transportation by vessel carriers.
  • Providing traditional freight forwarding services (booking, space management, transport management).

Key Compliance Requirements (Security/IT):

  • Maintain security protocols, including appropriate physical and cyber security measures.
  • Safeguard all written, verbal, and electronic information provided by Colgate.
  • Implement industry-standard cyber-security procedures to mitigate shipping information exposure.

3.2. Transportation Service Levels (KPIs)

The FF must perform Transportation Services across all lanes and Cost Models in accordance with the following minimum service level requirements:

Core Reliability Targets (85% Gross Minimum)

  • Booking to Sailing Week: 85% On-time (Bookings requested sailing week vs. actual sailing week, plus 7 days buffer).
  • On-Time Pickups: 85% On-time (Pick date requested vs. actual pick up date, plus 7 days buffer).
  • Segment Adherence: 85% On-time (Pick-up to Origin Port, Port intra nodal, Port to Delivery, etc. against CM SLA).
  • On-Time Deliveries: 85% On-time (Actual CM delivery date vs. SLA).

Key Compliance & Reporting

  • Booking Acceptance: 99% (Bookings requested and accepted per container).
  • Provide Monthly Volume Evolution against CP's 12-month forecast by Lane ID and equipment type (TEU).
  • Provide Snapshot Reporting of TOP 30 Import/Export lanes by volume and spend.
  • Provide exception reporting with root cause analysis and corrective actions for worst-performing lanes.

3.3. Payment Terms and Billings

The invoicing process is strictly governed by the contract's financial and commercial terms.

  • Standard Payment Term: Net 60 days from the date of the invoice.
  • Invoice Validity: Invoices submitted after 90 days from the provision of the service are considered null and void.
  • Rate Alignment (RG): The CM Rate per Container (USD) found in the Routing Guide is the contracted price. FF must adhere to these rates.
  • Currency Payment: Charges are paid in Local Currency for local components (e.g., origin haulage) and USD for international components (Ocean Freight and BAF). The RG adjusts FX quarterly, ensuring local teams pay the current Local Currency equivalent.

Part III - SOP and Ownership

Establishing clear roles, change management, and financial governance for the RG.

9. Roles and Responsibilities: Shipper vs. Destination (SOP Timing)

The Destination team bears the primary responsibility for the Routing Guide's ownership and accuracy, while the Shipper team focuses on operational adherence and cost control.

Destination (Importer / Buyer) - RG OWNER

The destination market owns the lane data and is responsible for initiating and managing change.

  • RG Ownership: Holds sole responsibility for the accuracy and maintenance of the Routing Guide data.
  • New FF Onboarding: Conducts and manages the entire operational setup for new Freight Forwarders using the Import Operational SOP Checklist.
  • Change Requests: Initiates and manages all change requests via the APAC Request Log File (see Section 12).
  • Escalation & Approval: Reviews and provides the final sign-off on all Spot Rates and Mode Changes (e.g., Ocean to Air) requested by the Origin.
  • Volume Forecasting: Must update and submit Annual Volume for the following year’s global bidding, using the Division's Q4 analysis.
  • Compliance Note: Volume fluctuations from the RG can lead to payment of SPOT rates or lost space allocation.

Origin (Shipper / Seller) - RG OPERATOR

The shipper's role is execution, adherence, and immediate reporting of deviations from the RG.

  • Operational Adherence: Must exclusively follow the rates, FFs, and Cost Models documented in the RG.
  • Rate Compliance: Ensures the FF invoices align with the contracted RG CM Rate and local charges.
  • Escalation Point: Reports any operational issues (e.g., service failure, booking problems) to the Destination Owner.
  • Spot Rate Initiation: Submits requests for Spot rates or Ocean-to-Air changes to the Destination Owner for required approval.

Routing Guide Publication & Validation Timeline

Day 1: Global publishes the new RG version (1st day of the month or earlier).
Day 2: Division publishes the Division's copy and the RG Change Management Log file.
Day 2 - 5: Destination teams check their lanes against the new version.
Change Required: Destination teams must use the RG Change Management Log file to populate all requested changes.
Day 5: Global checks the submitted log file and incorporates changes into the next RG version.
Urgent Rate Changes: Destination raises urgent issues via email to Division, who works with Global to fix and communicate directly to FFs/Shippers for immediate invoice correctness.

10. Escalation Process: Rate Variances, SPOT, and Mode Changes

Deviations from the contracted RG rate require formal documentation and sign-off, primarily owned by the Destination team.

Rate Variances (Invoice Audit)

If an FF invoice rate exceeds the RG CM Rate, the Shipper must immediately notify the Destination Owner. The Destination Owner will investigate and validate the charge with Global Transportation.

SPOT Rate Requests

SPOT rates are approved only if stocks are needed or there are risks on inventory. The Origin requests the rate from the FF, but approval is managed through Local Logistics Managers and CS&L Directors at Destination before booking is confirmed.

Ocean to Air Mode Change

Requires the highest level of approval. The Origin must propose, but the change must be fully validated and approved by the Destination Owner, often requiring Global Transportation sign-off due to cost implications.

11. SOP: New Freight Forwarder Onboarding

The onboarding of a new FF is driven by the Destination (Import) team, particularly for IT and data requirements, using the official checklists.

IMPORTER (Destination) Checklist Focus

  • Vendor Setup: Initiate new vendor setup with local CP Finance/CBS.
  • Credit Terms: Confirm 45-day credit terms with the FF as per the Global Contract.
  • Receiving SOP: Explain current DC receiving process and capacity SOP to the FF.
  • Track & Trace: Share detailed track & trace requirements and required reporting formats.
  • EDI/IT: Define required EDI linkages, formats (if non-TM9), and systems interfacing.

EXPORTER (Origin) Checklist Focus

  • FF Vendor Setup: Assist with local vendor paperwork (tax ID, local registration).
  • Booking Process: Explain the current export booking process (the "As Is" process) to the FF.
  • Organigram Share: Provide the FF with the local company logistics organigram (contact persons).
  • FF Documentation: Ensure FF provides their updated local country contact list.
  • Documentation: Confirm all export documentation requirements with the new FF.

12. RG Management, Change, and Funding the Growth

Ownership of change management ensures cost control and prevents recurrence of large financial deviations.

Requesting Changes on the Routing Guide

The Destination team manages all requests for changing RG parameters (rates, free time, FFs, etc.).

  • Process: Destination teams populate all required changes using the APAC Request Log File.
  • Timing: Changes are submitted by the Destination team to Global/Division between Day 2 and Day 5 of the publication cycle.
  • Content: Document the Detail from the Current RG and the Requested Change for every field impacted.
  • Accountability: Destination Owner is accountable for validation before implementation in the RG.

Management of Ocean Freight Funding the Growth

Financial planning hinges on RG data and accurate volume forecasting, managed by the Destination team.

  • Savings Calculation: Destination calculates savings by comparing the Cost of Sales budget against actual shipments dispatched (containers) and the Cost Model utilized.
  • Savings Status Check: On a **quarterly basis**, Destination must check the status of their Funding the Growth Program and submit it to the Division for database update.

13. Automated Routing Guide: Process and Application of New Lanes

The Automated RG is the primary hybrid SAP-powered portal for all rate requests, ensuring real-time data integrity and reducing manual errors.

Types of Requests

All requests must pass through the portal, categorized by intent and shipment volume:

  • FCL Long Term: Full container loads, moving ≥ 12 containers/year. Requires fixed price/guaranteed service.
  • FCL Short Term (Spot): Full container loads, moving 1 to 11 containers/year. Typically requested for two months.
  • FCL Study Lane: Movements in study, not yet finalized, managed by network operations/procurement for potential future lanes.
  • LCL & Air Shipments: Not currently managed by the Global Transportation Automated RG.

Step-by-Step System Usage (Requester)

  1. Access the Portal: Obtain access via a CARS request on Our-Colgate, adding the role G:ZZ:RGA:REQUESTER in SAP/TRP.
  2. Basic Information Setup: Ensure required data (Ports, Unlocodes) is available in the system before creating the request.
  3. Create Rate Request: Select the correct purpose type (Long Term, Short Term/Spot, or Study Lane).
  4. Enter Lane Details: Populate all necessary fields including Origin, Destination, Equipment Type (e.g., 40HC), and the Annual Volume (Container) for the lane.
  5. Submission: Submit the request to trigger the FF notification for pricing. The status changes to 'In Process (FF)'.
  6. Monitoring: Track the request status in your worklist and check for Final Approval by Global Logistics.

RG Request Status Codes (Summary)

Draft: Request created but not yet submitted.
In Process (FF): FF is currently providing the required rate.
Approved: Rate has been validated by Global and is set to be published.
Rejected: Request not approved (e.g., incomplete data, non-standard request).

14. Hub Guidance: Reviewing Ocean Freight Bid Responses

Hubs will receive a recommended award overview from the tender file, which will likely be based on cost alone. The following checklist is crucial for a comprehensive review before making a final award decision.

Key Checks Before Finalizing Awards

🏷️

Verify Cost Model Columns

Ensure costs are present in the correct columns according to your model (e.g., the "export haulage" column has costs for a DP cost model).

🚫

Check for Blank Columns

Randomly check that columns are not left blank. A "zero" value indicates a deliberate bid, whereas an empty cell could be an oversight.

🚢

Consolidate FFs per Route

Avoid awarding different container types on the same route to different forwarders (e.g., 20GP to FF A and 40GP to FF B). Aim for one forwarder per route.

⚖️

Confirm CM and Incoterm

Do the awarded cost model and incoterm meet your requirements as specified in the current Routing Guide?

💰

Validate D2D Costs

The Door-to-Door (D2D) cost per container must be at least the same as the sum of the individual CM costs per container. If not, there is a calculation error.

⏱️

Review Free Time

Are the offered detention and demurrage free times the same as or better than your current practice?

🧭

Check Transit/Routing

Are the proposed transit times and routing aligned with your current operational requirements?

📍

Verify Ports

Confirm that the specified ports of origin and destination (UNLOCODE) are correct for the lanes you requested.

❓

Check for Missing Lanes

Verify that you are not missing any lanes versus what you requested in your 2026 volume data gathering file.

Part IV - Spotting Opportunities

Proactive strategies for cost reduction and operational excellence.

15. External Benchmarking and Market Insights

Utilizing external data platforms is essential for validating the competitiveness of your contracted rates and identifying savings potential.

Usage of Xeneta for Rate Validation

Xeneta provides global market pulse data, allowing managers to compare CP's contracted CM rates against live market standards.

  • Objective: Validate if current RG rates are within competitive market range (short-term and long-term).
  • Action: If the RG rate is significantly above the market high, raise the issue via the APAC Request Log for renegotiation/review.
  • Benefit: Ensures cost of sales remains competitive and prevents overpaying on ocean freight.

RG Comparison Points

Always check the D2D benchmark rates (RG Column AJ) to evaluate the efficiency of local haulage costs.

  • CM Rate (AI): The contracted rate paid to the FF for the defined scope.
  • D2D Rate (AJ): The hypothetical cost if the FF handled the full door-to-door service.
  • Opportunity: If the combined CM + Local Haulage cost exceeds the D2D rate, it signals inefficiency in local haulage that requires optimization.

16. Container and Shipment Utilization

Driving utilization directly reduces the number of containers dispatched, providing immediate cost reduction (since freight is container-based).

Maximizing Container Utilization

The less container space wasted, the greater the savings. Focus on maximizing weight or cube.

  • Principle: Lesser containers dispatched = Lesser cost. (Direct correlation with annual volume and spend).
  • Action: Work with Production Teams to ensure packaging configuration and loading sequence maximize container capacity close to the maximum Gross Weight (RG Col CH).
  • Reporting: Monitor the Container Utilization Report (KPI 3.2) to track performance.

Optimizing Containers Per Shipment (BL)

Consolidating multiple smaller orders into a single, larger shipment reduces fixed documentation and handling costs.

  • Alignment: Align with Planning Teams on stock prioritization to build full vessel loads.
  • Coordination: Align with Freight Forwarders (shipment departure) and Production Teams (stock availability) to meet optimized cut-offs.
  • Metric: Aim to increase the average number of containers per Bill of Lading (BL) dispatched on key lanes.

17. Free Time Optimization and Process Alignment

Free time is a cost-lever. Optimization must be aligned with Inventory Models to translate operational efficiency into financial benefits.

Optimize Local Processes for Free Time Use

Ensure local operational processes (customs, receiving, dispatch) are faster than the contracted free time.

  • Destination Focus: Accelerate customs clearance and warehouse receiving processes.
  • Origin Focus: Optimize export clearance and dispatching lead times.
  • Inventory Alignment: Ensure the optimized lead times are incorporated into the Planning Inventory Models.

Reducing Free Time = Better Rates

As operational processes become robust, reduce the requested free time to leverage better ocean freight quotations.

  • Principle: Free time is a negotiated service cost. Longer free times increase the carrier's risk/cost (container idle time), which is passed to CP.
  • Action: Once operations are consistently optimized, formally request a reduction in free time via the MOC process (Section 12) to demand lower FF costs.
  • Benefit: Translates operational improvements directly into reduced Ocean Freight Quotations.

4 KEY BOTTLENECKS IDENTIFIED

These four operational friction points were responsible for the majority of the delays, adding valuable days to the overall lead time.

⏳

Slow D/T Approval

Formal, internal finance approval for tax computation caused unnecessary lag.

📑

Late Documents

Source plants provided essential shipping documents too late in the process.

💸

Manual Cash Settlement

Manual cash payments for carrier charges added 1-2 days waiting time.

🛑

Late Endorsements

Strict cut-off times (3 PM) for brokers delayed delivery orders to the next day.

4 KEY SOLUTIONS: PROACTIVE FRONT-LOADING

The strategy was to shift all documentation, payments, and endorsements to the "Over Water" stage, transforming the process from sequential to parallel by addressing the stakeholder delays directly.

🗂️

CP Export

Mandate complete documents 2 days prior to vessel arrival.

📧

CP Finance

Formal approval **eliminated**. Tax computation is now email notification only.

💳

Freight Forwarder

Leverages **credit line** to bypass cash settlement delays and provides charges 48hrs in advance.

⏱️

Brokers

Must complete all settlement and documentation within 24 hours of advance notice.
